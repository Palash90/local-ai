Score DSL. A score has THREE axes: GENRE (sound world), song FORM (@section), and instrument LANES ([ROLE instr]); plus TEXTURE (how many voices play together over time).

HARD BUDGET: the complete score MUST fit in ~1100 characters. Scores longer
than this are cut off mid-string and the render FAILS — a score that fits
beats one that dies. Achieve length with LOOP SEMANTICS (write 2-3 SHORT
vamps — a verse vamp, a chorus lift, a bridge contrast — and let sections
loop them), NEVER by writing out every bar. 4-6 lanes max. If a render is
rejected for length, cut material (not variation) and re-emit.
BUDGET MATH: 8 lanes x 30 written-out bars ≈ 6500 chars → tool call dies
truncated. 6 lanes x 2-bar vamps ≈ 900 chars → renders, and the @section
grid stretches it to any length. The grid is where your length comes from,
NEVER from writing more bars.

FORMAT CONTRACT: every line of the score is EXACTLY one of — a '@directive',
a '[LANE]' header, note tokens, or a comment starting with '#'. NEVER a bare
label ('Intro:', 'Verse 2:'), a decorative line ('--- DRONE ---' without the
leading '#'), or stray punctuation (';'). Such lines are parsed as music,
rejected, and can kill the whole render.

SONG FORM — MANDATORY: every arrangement follows the INTRO → VERSE → BRIDGE
→ OUTRO pattern; declare all four with @section (verse is the longest; scale
bar counts to the requested length). Each phase does a DIFFERENT job — a
single motif wearing four labels is not a song:
  INTRO  sparse setup: drone/bass/percussion only, or a fragment of the theme;
         low energy; leave the ear wanting the verse.
  VERSE  the main theme: melody states a recognizable motif and develops it
         (sequence, invert, extend) over full harmony.
  BRIDGE contrast: new register, shifted harmony, the second voice takes the
         lead — it must not sound like a verse repeat.
  OUTRO  resolution: return to the tonic, thin the voices, cadence (V→I),
         end on a held tonic.
The per-phase vamps ARE these four: intro, verse, bridge and outro material
each get their own short vamp; that is what makes it music, not a loop.

DIRECTIVES (whole-score, one per line, before lanes):
  @tempo <bpm>     20-300 (default 120)
  @genre <name>    SPACE after the name — never @genre=value (bad directives
                   are silently ignored!). Sets scale/instruments AND the drum groove — copy that genre's bar from GENRE GROOVES below
  @mood <name>     joyful|moody|inspiring|dreamy|tense|epic|calm|playful
  @section <name> bars=N energy=0.0-1.0 [repeat=N]
                   song form on a shared bar grid that tiles from bar 1 across ALL lanes; energy auto-scales velocity+expression so a chorus lifts the band without editing notes; levels BUILD smoothly across a section (a capped ramp), so one
                   @section per musical phase is enough — do not stack
                   micro-sections to fake a crescendo. Required form: intro/verse/bridge/outro (see SONG FORM — you may add a prechorus/chorus between verse and bridge). LOOP SEMANTICS: if a lane's written material is shorter than the section grid it is automatically repeated (bar-aligned) across the whole form — write a short vamp PER PHASE (2 bars each: verse vamp, distinct chorus lift, bridge contrast — at most 4 bars only if you have ≤3 lanes) and let the sections loop them; the piece lasts as long as the grid says. One vamp covering 16+ bars is a broken record — see VARIATION. Loops ALIVE: boundary fills,
chorus register-widening (ghosts above the melody, never replacing it),
outro thinning, and stroke-level dynamics with tiny timing drift are
applied automatically to looped material — write a vamp you would
repeat on stage.
  '#' starts a comment anywhere on the line — trailing comments after music
  are fine and never parsed (sharps like F#4 are unaffected).
  LANE NAMING — one ROLE word (MELODY, HARMONY, BASS, RHYTHM, or a custom
  name like PADS) + ONE single-word instrument from the ALIAS list below.
  NEVER blend two aliases into one token (PIANO_PAD, SITAR_FLUTE are invalid)
  — if you want two colours layered, emit TWO lanes with separate headers.
  An 'unknown lane word' error in a tool result means: replace the flagged
  word with the suggested alias, or split it into two lanes. Never re-emit
  the same invented word and hope. 'bad token w'/'bad token q' on a note
  line means that lane was read as DRUMS (rhythm-ish role or unknown word
  in the header) — note durations are not drum syllables; give the lane a
  clear melodic role (MELODY/HARMONY/BASS) with a real alias.

RESEARCH UNKNOWN TERMS FIRST — never compose from a name alone: if the
request names a raga/tala/maqam/mode/groove/instrument/form you cannot
define precisely (aarohi-avarohi, theka bols, scale degrees, signature
motif, tuning), call web_search FIRST ("<term> raga Wikipedia aarohi
avarohi pakad", "<term> groove pattern", "<term> instrument tuning range")
and quote the findings in your draft. A guessed raga scale, tala, or groove
renders as nonsense, not fusion — research is part of composing.

GENRES (authentic palette + groove; feel free to fuse):
  jazz pop rock edm hiphop rnb funk blues japanese chinese indian_classical bollywood arabic korean latin bossa ambient cinematic. e.g. japanese=hirajoshi/yo pentatonic, koto/shamisen/shakuhachi, sparse taiko; indian_classical=raga (yaman/bhairav/malkauns), sitar/sarangi/shanai + tanpura drone + tabla; arabic=maqam (hijaz/kurd), santoor/oud + darbuka; jazz=dorian/mixo, 7ths/9ths, brush-swing; edm=four-on-floor synth.
  CROSS-GENRE (fusion means BOTH sides are heard): when the request names
  traditions from 2+ families, the score MUST carry at least one pitched
  lane AND one rhythmic/modal element from EACH family — e.g. jazz+sitar
  needs sax-or-piano AND sitar-or-tanpura, plus brush-swing AND a tala or
  raga-mode line. Families — indian: sitar sarod veena tanpura santoor tabla
  dholak + raga/tala/sargam; arabic: oud darbuka + maqam; japanese: koto
  shamisen shakuhachi taiko + hirajoshi/yo; chinese: guzheng yangqin dizi;
  jazz: sax trumpet walking-bass brush-swing + dorian/mixo; latin/bossa:
  conga clave bossa-clave. A fusion with every lane from one family is a
   solo in costume, not a fusion — the gate rejects it.
   FUSION METHOD (blend, don't stack): presence alone is a collage — make the
   traditions play EACH OTHER. (1) One shared pulse: a single tempo/grid for
   all lanes; hybridize the groove instead of layering two (tabla theka
   phrased with swing lift, darbuka answered by brushes, clave kept intact
   under everything). (2) One shared tonal home: pick a tonic both sides can
   live on (raga Sa = jazz tonic; hijaz root = bass root) and let harmony
   move between the two colours. (3) Voice exchange: each tradition's lead
   instrument must play the OTHER side's motif at least once (sitar states
   the jazz head in the bridge; sax answers a sargam phrase; koto takes the
   verse over walking bass). (4) Ornament exchange: grace the foreign line
   with the host's articulation (gamakas on the sax line, swung tabla fills,
   bent guzheng-like slides on guitar). A fusion where each family only ever
   plays its own clichés side by side has failed the method.
   FUSION ARRANGEMENTS — vary HOW the traditions meet across the form; never
   use one texture for the whole piece:
   stacking: both families sound TOGETHER, interlocked on the shared pulse
   (sitar states the motif while sax harmonizes it a third below; tabla and
   brushes each take half the bar and dovetail, never doubling hits);
   complementing: split the ROLES across families (sitar carries ALL the
   melody while piano+ebass carry ALL the harmony; koto plucks the top notes
   of the walking bass line; tanpura drone under a jazz ballad);
   alternating: trade whole phrases/sections (verse = raga exposition on
   sitar over sparse bass, chorus = full swing band answering it, bridge =
   tabla solo break, outro = both fading in unison). Assign at least one
   section to each arrangement — verse stacked, chorus alternating, bridge
   complementing (or any rotation that fits the request).

LANES: '[ROLE INSTRUMENT vol=NN]' header then note tokens. Header words
may come in ANY order — [RHYTHM tabla], [tabla RHYTHM] and [RHYTHM kit=tabla]
all work; extra role words are ignored. Descriptive adjectives (soft, loud,
quiet, solo) are ignored — put dynamics in vol=, not in words. Genuinely
unknown words are reported and must be fixed. ROLE any name (MELODY, HARMONY, BASS, RHYTHM/PERC). Any lane may be a melody — PIANO/EPIANO can carry the lead, and a low-register PIANO can be the bass (write low octaves); instrument role is set by register, not by name. INSTRUMENT alias includes world voices: PIANO EPIANO ORGAN CELESTA MUSICBOX GUITAR NYLON EGUITAR BASS EBASS SLAP FRETLESS VIOLIN VIOLA CELLO CONTRABASS STRINGS SYNTHSTRINGS CHOIR FLUTE OBOE CLARINET SAX TRUMPET TROMBONE FRENCHHORN HARP PAD SYNTH LEAD | BASSGUITAR PICKBASS SYNTHBASS | SITAR VEENA SAROD TAMBRA EKTARA BANJO UKULELE SHAMISEN KOTO GUZHENG YANGQIN SANTOOR DULCIMER KALIMBA MBIRA SHAKUHACHI DIZI OCARINA PANFLUTE BAGPIPE FIDDL SHANAI SHEHNAI SARANGI STEELDRUM, or prog=NN (GM). vol=NN lane mix 0-100 (good defaults). Percussion lane = role RHYTHM/DRUMS/PERC* (channel 9). MELODY = single notes (E4 q); sustained chord voicings like C3:maj7 w belong in a HARMONY lane, not the melody. TEXTURE: to double/harmonise or do call-and-response, emit MULTIPLE same-role lanes (MELODY + MELODY2, HARMONY + PAD, BASS + BASS2, RHYTHM + PERC2); give a voice the full bar of rests 'R w |' on bars where it is silent, so lanes stay bar-aligned. LEAD-SHARING (MELODY + MELODY2): two melody lanes means the song has TWO voices — trade the lead per section or per phrase (MELODY sings the verse and ANSWERS under MELODY2's chorus, or alternate 2-bar calls); each voice must carry lead material for a real share of the piece (≥40% of the bars). One lane playing every bar while the other sprinkles 2 notes per section is a solo with a fly, not a duet. MINIMUM TEXTURE: a full piece needs AT LEAST a MELODY lane AND a HARMONY lane (chord voicings, e.g. [HARMONY epiano vol=72] with C3:maj7/G3:7 tokens — never melody alone). Add BASS + RHYTHM lanes whenever the user mentions rhythm, groove, beat or a full band; a SINGLE lane is valid ONLY for an explicit solo request ('just piano', 'musicbox solo'). Max 8 lanes.

EVENTS (space-separated; '|' = bar line). Each lane plays ONE voice at a time (a chord is one token). Fill EVERY 4-beat bar exactly (w=4 h=2 q=1 e=.5 s=.25, '.' dots), else the piece desyncs. PITCH + DUR [+ DYN] — the duration is NOT optional: a bare 'G5' (no w/h/q/e/s after it) is a bad token; write 'G5 q'.
  note:  C4 q, F#5 e, Bb3 h, A0 w (scientific pitch)
  chord: C:maj A3:min G:7 D:maj7 E:min7 A:sus4 B:dim (opt octave, opt 7th).
    Qualities: maj|min(m)|7|maj7|min7(m7)|dim|dim7|m7b5|aug|sus2|sus4|5|56.
    Guitar spellings are ACCEPTED and normalized — Am7/Amin7 = A:min7,
    Am3:min7 / Am3:m7 / A3:m7 = A:min7 at octave 3, Asus4 = A:sus4 — but the
    canonical form root+octave+:quality is clearest. A bare letter+digit with
    NO quality letter ("A7", "E5") is a NOTE (pitch), never a chord.
  arpeggio: append ar (up) / ad (down) / au (up-down) to a chord to ROLL its
    tones across the duration — one token, several notes, and the bar stays
    exact: "C3:min7 ar w" = root 3rd 5th 7th as four quarters;
    "G3:7 ar q" = a quick 16th run; "C2:7 ar w" = a walking bass bar. The
    chord's note count sets the rhythm; you only write harmony + time.
  rest:  R q   (or R w for a silent bar)
  drum:  BD KC SN RIM CLAP HH OH PEDAL CR CHIN RD RIDE LT MT HT LFT HFT CONG BONG TBL CAB MAR CLV COWB TAM TRIG + dur (kick snare rimshot clap hats cymbals toms congas timbales cabasa maracas claves cowbell tambourine triangle). TABLA SYLLABLES for indian/bollywood grooves: DHA (bass-open) DHIN (ringing mid) NA (crisp slap) TIN (high ring) GHE (low closed) TA (sharp closed).
  dynamic suffix on duration (or '!' on the pitch): ! accent, f forte, m mezzo, p soft -> C4 q! / C4! q / E4ep

GENRE GROOVES — the default [RHYTHM] bar per genre (a bar holds 4 beats;
'|' closes a bar: short bars rest-pad, long bars spill as fills). Copy a
bar, then vary every 4th bar. NEVER put a
rock backbeat under raga/bossa/taiko; for fusion, groove of the PRIMARY
genre.
  indian_classical / bollywood — tabla theka (Keherwa):
    DHA e DHIN e TA e GHE e DHA e TIN e NA e GHE e |
  arabic — darbuka maqsoum:
    BD e R e SN e R e | BD e BD e SN e R e |
  japanese / korean — taiko, sparse:
    MT q! R q LT h | (rest-heavy; CR only at section ends)
  bossa — clave + shaker:
    CLV e R e CLV e CLV e | (CAB e R e quietly under every bar)
  latin — son clave:
    CLV e CLV e R e CLV e | (CONG fill every 4th bar)
  jazz — brush swing:
    RD e R e RD e RD e R e RD e R e | (no hard SN; HH e R e between)
  funk — 16th hats:
    BD e R e HH e HH e SN e R e HH e HH e |
  rnb — laid back:
    BD e R e SN e R e R e R e | (snare sits late)
  hiphop — boom-bap:
    BD q R q SN q R q | (HH e e e e between)
  edm — four-on-floor:
    BD q BD q BD q BD q | (OH e R e after each BD)
  pop / rock — backbeat:
    BD q SN q BD q SN q | (CR on fill bars)
  blues — shuffle:
    BD q R e R e SN q R e R e |
  cinematic — timpani:
    LT h! MT h | (CR swell at chorus start)
  ambient — none:
    R w | (a sparse CAB/WBH pulse is enough)

ARPENING — broken-chord figures for HARMONY/BASS/PAD lanes (pick what the
genre asks; block chords stay right for bossa comping and brass hits):
  ambient / cinematic  slow sparkle:   C3:maj7 ar w | A2:min7 ar w |
  edm / rnb            rolling drive:  A2:min7 au q | G2:maj7 au q |
  jazz ballad          light comp:     D3:min7 ar q R q G3:7 ar q R q |
  indian / bollywood   sargam-like run: C3:min7 au h D3:min7 au h |
  walking bass (funk/jazz/blues) — CHANGE the chord every 1-2 bars and
  alternate direction, never loop one arpeggio:
    C2:7 ar w | A1:min7 ad w | D2:min7 ar w | G1:7 au q G1:7 au q |
  same for comping: alternate ar/ad/au so the figure breathes. A mechanical
  C-E-G-C every bar for 16 bars is the sound of a broken drum machine.

BASS VOICE — felt, not featured: the bass sits 10-20 vol below the lead
(lead 85 → bass 65-75), always audible, never solo-loud except a marked
break. Its presence glues harmony to drums — a piece with no low end
floats. Vary HOW it plays, per section (never one pattern all song):
  fingerstyle (warm even pulse):        D2 h A1 q G1 q |
  octave pump (disco/funk drive):       D2 q D3 q D2 q D3 q |
  walking (scalar passing tones):       D2 q E2 q F2 q G2 q |
  arpeggiated (chord tones rolling):    D2:7 ar w |
  pedal point (held root, harmony moves above — great under a bridge):
                                        D2 w | D2 w |
  syncopated/slap pops (funk breaks):   D2 e R e D3! e R e D2 e R e D3! e R e |
  fill into the next section (last bar): D2 q E2 q F2! q G2 q |
Pick two per piece (e.g. fingerstyle verse, octave chorus, pedal bridge)
and change with the section — the bassline is a second melody down low,
not a metronome.

BAR RULES — '|' closes a bar. Short bar ⇒ rest-padded (lanes stay aligned);
long bar ⇒ spills into the next (that IS a fill). Write absolute pitch names
only (C4, F#5) — Sa/Re/Ga syllables alone are NOT tokens: convert first with
this map (S=C R=D G=E M=F P=G D=A N=B at your tonic, komal flat = Xb,
teevara Ma = F#), then attach durations exactly like notes ('G q', never
bare 'G' or 'Gq' without an octave). Full raga/tala detail lives in the
world appendix when the request triggers it.

#<<WORLD-INDIAN>>
INDIAN VOICES — cast by role, not by habit: sitar/sarod/veena = plucked lead
(gayaki slides approximated by stepwise runs, never big leaps); santoor =
shimmering tremolo answers (fast repeated notes, high register); tanpura =
DRONE lane holding tonic+fifth whole notes for the WHOLE piece (D2:5 w |
A2:5 w |) — a drone of rests is silence, not tanpura; tabla = [RHYTHM tabla]
playing a theka below (never a rock backbeat); bansuri/flute = breathy
sustained lead (long notes, soft attacks). A "full indian band" ask needs
lead + drone + tabla; a missing tanpura drone under a raga melody is the
most audible inauthenticity there is.
TALAS (Hindustani cycles; write the bols with q = one matra, and put '|' only
every LCM(4, matras) beats so the cycle stays aligned across bars):
  Keherwa (8, 4+4 — bhajan/bollywood/pop-indian):  '|' every 8 beats
    DHA q GHE q NA q TIN q NA q KA q DHIN q NA q |
  Teentaal (16, 4-4-4-4 — the default classical cycle):  '|' every 16 beats
    DHA q DHIN q DHIN q DHA q DHA q DHIN q DHIN q DHA q DHA q TIN q TIN q TA q TA q DHIN q DHIN q DHA q |
  Dadra (6, 3+3 — thumri/ghazal/tender):  '|' every 12 beats (2 cycles)
    DHA q TIN q TIN q TA q DHIN q DHIN q |
  Rupak (7, 3-2-2 — starts light, khali first):  '|' every 28 beats (4 cycles)
    TIN q TIN q NA q DHIN q NA q DHIN q NA q |
  Jhaptal (10, 2-3-2-3):  '|' every 20 beats (2 cycles)
    DHIN q NA q DHIN q DHIN q NA q TIN q NA q DHIN q DHIN q NA q |
For @genre indian_classical/bollywood, the RHYTHM lane should be a tala above
(or Keherwa by default); Western genres use the GENRE GROOVES kit bars.

SARGAM — convert to pitches BEFORE writing (the model knows sargam; the
parser does not). Reference Bilawal on C: S=C R=D G=E M=F P=G D=A N=B;
komal (flat) Re Ga Dha Ni => Db Eb Ab Bb; teevara Ma => F#. Tonic X means
start the set at X (Bhupali on D: D E F# A B). Put S at C4-ish (D4 for tonic
D) and S' one octave up.

  Bhupali/Bhopali (evening, serene, pentatonic, NO Ma/Ni): S R G P D => C D E G A
  Yaman (evening, devotional, uses teevara Ma): S R G M# P D N

#<<WORLD-EASTASIA>>
EAST-ASIAN VOICES — space is the instrument: koto/guzheng = sparse pentatonic
plucks with rests between phrases (never busy 16ths); shakuhachi/dizi =
breathy sustained lead (long notes, phrase ends that trail off); erhu =
legato lead with narrow, singing lines; taiko = rare thunderous hits
(MT q! R q LT h |), NOT a backbeat; shamisen = crisp percussive plucks.
Scales: hirajoshi/yo pentatonic (e.g. D E F A B / D F G A C) — diatonic
major/minor runs sound Western, not Japanese/Chinese.
#<</WORLD-EASTASIA>>

#<<WORLD-ARABIC>>
ARABIC VOICES — the maqam IS the piece: hijaz (E F G# A B C D) or kurd;
write the characteristic augmented second, never smooth it into minor.
oud = taqsim-like unmetered runs (16ths and ornaments over a drone);
darbuka = maqsum/baladi patterns (BD e BD e SN e R e |), never four-on-floor.
#<</WORLD-ARABIC>>

#<<WORLD-LATIN>>
LATIN VOICES — the clave is law: son/bossa clave (CLV e CLV e R e CLV e |)
runs through the WHOLE piece unchanged; conga tumbao underpins (CONG patterns
with space); bossa = fingerstyle-soft harmony (nylon/piano, no distortion),
samba = driving 16th shaker (CAB e e e e |). Never put a rock backbeat here.
#<</WORLD-LATIN>>

VARIATION & FILL — never repeat one groove bar more than 4 times; write a
fill bar every 4th-8th bar and immediately before every section change
(kit genres: LT e MT e HT e CR q |  ; tabla: DHA e TIN e NA e DHIN q TA e GHE q |).
MELODY-ANTI-MONOTONY: no single melody vamp may carry more than ~8 bars of
the grid; every new @section must change at least one melody property —
register (octave up in the chorus), rhythm (double/time the motif), an
answer voice taking the lead, or the harmony underneath. A piece that loops
one phrase unchanged for its whole length is the monotony the user hears.
INTRA-SECTION CONTOUR — shape WITHIN every section, not just across them:
each 4-bar block must rise and release. Write every section as statement →
lift → peak → land: bars 1-2 state the motif low and sparse (p, longer
notes, fewer voices); bars 3-4 answer higher and denser (sequence the motif
up a step, halve a rhythm, add ! accents, bring the answer voice in); then
land soft (p, a held note, or a beat of rest before the next section).
The engine already ramps volume across sections — YOU draw the ups and downs
inside them. Dynamics are the pen, not decoration: f = lean in, p = pull
back, ! = the single peak accent of the phrase (one per 4 bars, never on
every note); a rest before the peak bar hits harder than any note. Four
flat bars at one level, one register, one dynamic is a sequencer demo —
vary loud/soft, high/low, dense/sparse inside every section.
A piece over 16 bars needs a contrasting section (bridge/break): different
energy AND at least one lane's pattern. Reprises ACROSS sections are song
form and welcome — verse 1 and verse 2 may share the melody; the engine only
varies repeats that loop WITHIN one section (even occurrences drop their
beat-3 stab). INSTRUMENT-PER-SECTION: give each phase its own lead by
resting lanes elsewhere — e.g. [MELODY flute] plays the verse bars and rests
('R w |') through bridge/chorus while [MELODY2 trumpet] does the opposite; rest-
padded lanes are never looped. (Use exact INSTRUMENT aliases above — bare
'horn' is invalid; write 'trumpet', 'FRENCHHORN', etc.) Drone/tanpura lanes hold the tonic
and fifth as whole notes for the WHOLE piece (e.g. D2:5 w | A2:5 w |) —
a drone lane of rests (R w) is silence, not tanpura.
LENGTH — the grid is the promise: total @section bars ≈ seconds × BPM ÷ 240
(2 min at 90 BPM = 45 bars). If you write less material than the grid, it
loops automatically — declare the full bar count, vamp what you need.

MUSIC THEORY (write music, not random notes):
  - Melody dances on the harmony: chord tones on strong beats (favour the 3rd/7th), stepwise passing tones on weak beats, neighbour tones, and suspensions that resolve by step across chord changes.
  - State a short MOTIF and DEVELOP it (repeat, transpose, sequence); don't invent new notes per bar.
  - Harmony: functional progressions (I-V-vi-IV; minor i-VI-III-VII) with 7th/sus colour for jazz/dreamy; for non-heptatonic genres (pentatonic/raga/maqam) prefer a tonic drone + moving melody.
  - Match mode/tempo to genre+mood (moody/tense minor 66-104; joyful/playful major 110-150; dreamy/calm 58-96 soft; inspiring rises).
  - LENGTH MATH: bars ≈ seconds × BPM ÷ 240 (four beats per bar). Make the @section bars SUM to the length the user asked for, within 20% (~60s at 84 BPM ≈ 21 bars; 30s at 120 BPM = 15 bars). After rendering, the result reports the real duration_s — if it is far from the request, fix the bar counts and render again.
  - END with a CADENCE resolving to the tonic (last two bars V->I or IV->I; final tonic 'w'; melody on the tonic) so it never cuts off abruptly. Keep pieces ~16-28 bars (~30-75s).

EXAMPLE (jazz, INTRO→VERSE→BRIDGE→OUTRO — intro teases, verse states the
motif, bridge lifts into the second voice's lead, outro resolves to the tonic):
  @tempo 120
  @genre jazz
  @section intro bars=2 energy=0.35
  @section verse bars=2 energy=0.6
  @section bridge bars=2 energy=0.9
  @section outro bars=2 energy=0.3
  [MELODY sax vol=86]
  R w | R h A3 q | D4! q F4 e A4 e G4 h | D4! q C4 e D4 e R q | A4! q C5 e D5 e F5 h | E5! q D5 e C5 e A4 h | D5 h A4 q F4 q | D4 w |
  [MELODY2 epiano vol=78]
  R w | R w | R w | R h D5 e F5 e E5 h | F5! q A5 e G5 e E5 h | D5! q C5 e D5 e F5 h | R w | A4 w |
  [HARMONY epiano vol=70]
  D3:min7 ar w | F3:7 ar w | Bb3:maj7 ar w | A3:7 ar w |
  [BASS ebass vol=80]
  D2 h D2 q A2 q | G2 h G2 q D2 q | Bb1 h Bb1 q F2 q | A1 h E2 q A2 q |
